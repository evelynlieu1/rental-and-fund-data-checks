# How reliable is the extracted text? (phase 2 review)

`src/2_pages.py` turned the 7 LF1 documents into 250 pages of text (`data/processed/pages.parquet`). On 8 Nov 2026 five reviewers compared that text with the real PDF pages: every page with a table, chart or number, and a sample of the rest. This file is what they found. Use it in phase 4 (check these pages against the image before recording a value) and phase 5 (send these pages to a layout-aware reader such as Amazon Textract, or treat values from them as low confidence).

## Page numbers: pdf_page against the number printed on the page

Always cite **pdf_page** (the page index in the file). To get the printed number:

| Document | Rule | Unnumbered pages |
|---|---|---|
| PDS (`pds`, 123 pages) | printed = pdf_page - 2, from pdf 4 to 121 | 1-3 (cover, inside cover, contents), 122 (blank), 123 (back cover) |
| LF1 annual report (`ar`, 44) | printed = pdf_page - 4, from pdf 6 to 43 | 1-5, 44; pdf 14, 19 and 43 are blank filler pages (record None) |
| Credit Fund annual report (`uar`, 48) | printed = pdf_page - 4, from pdf 6 to 47 | 1-5, 48; pdf 25 is image-only |
| TMD, monthly profile, roadshow deck | printed = pdf_page where a number shows | TMD: all 7 numbered; profile: 2-6; deck: 3-17 |
| FAQs | no printed numbers | all |

- **The documents' own cross-references use printed numbers.** "See page 29" in the Credit Fund report means pdf 33. Contents pages too.
- **Ignore the page numbers inside the EY audit letters** (ar pdf 37-40, uar pdf 44-46): they restart at 1.
- **The `printed_page` column in the parquet is wrong on about 15 pages.** The simple rule picked up chapter numbers, contents entries and stray footnote marks. Use the table above instead.

## Patterns to expect everywhere

- **Footnote marks fuse onto numbers:** "39%11", "Rate4 + 3.25%", "1.26x15", "31 March 202510". Strip them, and keep the footnote number: in the monthly profile it carries the as-at date.
- **Charts lose their labels:** donut and map percentages come out as a list without the names they belong to (PDS pdf 48 state map, pdf 51 borrowers, profile p4 and p5, deck p8 and p11). Pairing a label with a value from the text alone is unreliable.
- **Structure diagrams flatten:** the PDS diagram (pdf 18, 40) loses which responsible entity, custodian or manager box belongs to LF1 and which to the underlying funds. Take roles from the text sections instead.
- **Tables:** column headers often split from values, but row order mostly survives. Fee table (PDS pdf 80-86), annual report notes, and the Credit Fund ICR table (uar 17) need the image.

## Where a value could be read at the wrong level

The reviewers' notes on fees, limits and figures that belong to a different vehicle or date than they appear to. `schema.md` turns these into per-field traps.

- Structure changed after the PDS: the 26 May 2025 PDS describes LF1 as investing in TWO underlying funds (12 Month Term Account AND La Trobe US Private Credit Fund Class A, about 50:50, mandate 20-100% / 0-80%). It is easy to read LF1 as feeding only the 12MTA. This could not be checked from pages 1-62. If true, the PDS fee estimates (1.93% total, 0.07% performance fee), the leverage figure (1.60x look-through), the 39% LVR and the 5%-per-quarter US redemption terms all belong to a sleeve that may no longer exist. Do not compare them directly with the FY2026 annual report, Aug 2026 profile or Oct 2026 deck.
- Performance fee: the PDS (pdf 25) gives a 0.07% p.a. performance fee, but says LF1 does not charge one directly. It is charged inside LGAM Private Credit LLC (the US sleeve) and only flows through LF1's NAV. 'No performance fee' at LF1 level (ASX report) and '0.07% performance fees' in the PDS can both be true; label it underlying-fund level.
- Management fees and costs of 1.93% p.a. (pdf 25) is a total: only 0.05% is LF1's own RE fee plus 0.17% recoverable costs, and 1.71% is indirect costs inside the underlying funds. Do not report 1.93% as LF1's management fee, or 0.05% as the all-in cost.
- The 12MTA's 0.50% p.a. fee rebate to LF1 is only in a chart footnote (pdf 43). So 12MTA retail returns (e.g. 6.92% 1yr, pdf 49) are not what LF1 earns from the 12MTA. LF1 is a separate 'platform investor' class (pdf 24, 56).
- La Trobe Financial keeps 1.80% p.a. of 12MTA balances (pdf 49): a manager-level margin inside the underlying fund, funding an investor reserve held by the manager, not by the fund. It is not an LF1 fee line, but it drives the indirect-cost estimate.
- Capitalised 'Responsible Entity', 'Manager' and 'Fund' mean LF1's RE, Manager and LF1. Lower-case 'the responsible entity of the 12 Month Term Account / La Trobe US Private Credit Fund' is the same company acting for the underlying fund. On pdf 50-51 the chart footnotes say 'the Fund' or 'fund' but mean the US fund or LGAM LLC.
- Structure diagram (pdf 18, 40): right-hand boxes (Manager, RE, Custodian, Unit Registry) are LF1's; left-hand boxes (Investment Manager, RE, Custodian, Trustee) are the underlying funds'. The same firms appear in both, and the text extraction loses which side each box is on.
- Custodian: Perpetual holds LF1's units in the underlying funds, while the RE holds LF1's cash. Separately, Perpetual is custodian and mortgagee of the Australian Credit Fund's mortgage assets. These are different roles.
- AUM figures are at different levels: $13,030m (pdf 46) or 'c.$13 billion' (pdf 60) is the whole La Trobe Australian Credit Fund (7 accounts); $9,940.1m (pdf 48) is the 12MTA loan portfolio only; 'over $20bn' is the La Trobe Financial group. None of these is LF1's size (the offer was $100-300m).
- Concentration and size limits: '5% single asset / borrower' is a 12MTA scheme-level fact (pdf 17, 47). The LGAM LLC limits (1-3% per investment, 20% industry) are US-sleeve level. LF1 itself has only allocation ranges.
- '5%' means three different things: LF1's quarterly buy-back of 5% of LF1 units; the US fund's 5% of Class A units per quarter; LGAM LLC's 5% of interests per quarter. The 12MTA cap is 2% per month of LF1's own holding in the 12MTA (not 2% of the 12MTA).
- Loan counts: 11,540 loans is the 12MTA at 30 Apr 2025. LF1's 'more than 11,000 underlying credit assets' (pdf 17, 52) combines 12MTA loans with LGAM's 98 borrowers.
- Date mismatch inside the PDS: 12MTA data is at 30 Apr 2025, LGAM LLC data at 31 Mar 2025, credit scores at May 2025, the PDS is dated 26 May 2025, and the US asset allocation donut (pdf 50) has no date. Later documents are at 30 Jun 2026 (annual reports), 31 Aug 2026 (monthly profile) and Oct 2026 (deck), so PDS LVR 64.7% and loan counts are not current values.
- Same LVR, two values: 64.7% (text, pdf 20/21/45/46) vs 65% (chart, pdf 48), both at 30 Apr 2025 (rounding). The 80% figure is a portfolio maximum (limit), not an actual. The US 39% LVR is at a different date and level.
- Source-data inconsistency on pdf 48: the 12MTA LVR buckets sum to about $11,439m vs a $9,940.1m loan portfolio, and the percentages sum to about 104.5% ($4,405.8m is labelled 35.95% but would be about 44%). Flag it; do not treat it as an extraction error.
- Leverage: 'LF1 does not directly use leverage' and the '1.60x of NAV' look-through maximum (pdf 19, 57) are both true. The leverage sits in LGAM LLC (target 1.0x, cap 2.0x). The PDS wording '$1.60 of debt for every $1 of assets' is loose; do not record it as LF1 borrowing.
- Outperformance periods differ: 1.33% p.a. is since Oct 2002 (pdf 20, 45, 49), while 0.90/0.99/1.74% are the 1/3/5yr figures (pdf 49), all for the 12MTA, not LF1. The PDS historical chart (pdf 43) is a modelled blend, not actual LF1 returns.
- Manager fee wording conflicts: pdf 27 says no separate fee is payable to the Manager by the RE; pdf 61 says the RE pays the Manager a fee out of the RE's own remuneration. Board chair also conflicts: Donohoue is 'Executive Chairman' (pdf 26, 62), while Chersky's bio says he was appointed Non-Executive Chairman in Dec 2023 (pdf 62).
- THE PREMISE IS INCOMPLETE. This PDS says LF1 invests in TWO underlying funds: the 12 Month Term Account of the La Trobe Australian Credit Fund AND the La Trobe US Private Credit Fund Class A (pdf 65, 96, 120), with about 50% expected in the US fund (pdf 85-86). The FY2026 annual report (annual_report pdf 24, 32) shows both held at 30 Jun 2026: USPC $158.9m and 12MIA $110.2m, about 59/41 by my arithmetic. LF1 is not a feeder into the Australian account alone, so fee, risk, liquidity and valuation fields need a separate 'underlying' entry for each fund.
- PERFORMANCE FEE has three levels. LF1 charges none directly, the Australian Credit Fund charges none, and the US fund charges none at its own level (pdf 84). But LGAM Private Credit LLC, two levels below LF1, pays MS Capital Partners 12.5% of realised gains plus an income fee above a 5% annualised hurdle (pdf 85-86). The PDS still puts an estimated 0.07% p.a. of LF1 NAV into the fee table (0.58% if the waiver lapses). 'No outperformance fee' in ASX reports is true only at the LF1 level.
- The 0.07% performance-fee estimate assumes a 50% US weighting and is based on the previous financial year's ACTUAL LGAM fee, before LF1 existed (pdf 81, 85). The actual mix at 30 Jun 2026 is about 59% US, so later reported figures will not match.
- MANAGEMENT FEE figures sit on different bases. 0.05% is LF1's RE fee on LF1 NAV. 1.71% indirect and 1.93% total are % of LF1 NAV. 1.80% is the Australian account's own fee as a % of ITS average investor balances, less a 0.50% rebate to LF1 (pdf 84). 0.75% is the US fund fee on Class A NAV; 1.00% + 0.30% are at LGAM LLC. 1.25%, 5% and 5% are constitutional MAXIMA, not actuals. Never compare a maximum with an actual, or an underlying-base % with an LF1-NAV %.
- pdf 83 says an ASX buyer pays a full monthly RE fee 'and monthly Management Fee', yet the fee table has no LF1-level manager fee. The glossary (pdf 119-120) points 'Management Fee' and 'Responsible Entity Fee' to 10.3.3, which is actually Recoverable Costs (the RE fee is 10.3.2). Extraction may invent a direct LF1 management fee.
- pdf 83 also says the RE does not intend to recover normal costs at the PDS date, while the fee table includes estimated recoverable costs of 0.17% p.a. Flag this as an internal inconsistency rather than picking one.
- All PDS fee and cost figures are reasonable ESTIMATES for the FY ending 30 Jun 2025 (annualised) as at 26 May 2025, before the fund invested. Compare them with FY2026 annual-report actuals (to 30 Jun 2026) and the Aug/Oct 2026 profile and deck only as estimate-versus-actual across different years.
- Defined-term ambiguity. 'Responsible Entity', 'Manager' and 'Fund' (capitalised) mean LF1's RE (LFAM), LFS and LF1. But risk paragraphs on pdf 67-72 use lower-case 'the responsible entity' and 'investment manager' for the underlying funds' operators. pdf 69 even says a loan is 'regarded by the Fund as being in arrears', meaning the Australian account. 'Administrator' is LFS for LF1 but an MS entity for LGAM (pdf 111).
- LIQUIDITY. LF1 holders cannot redeem: they sell on the ASX or use buy-backs (pdf 74, 102). The suspension, 'not liquid', withdrawal-offer and 3.5% early-withdrawal-fee rules (pdf 68, 84) belong to the Australian account. The 5% quarterly repurchase limit and 14-day suspension notice (pdf 70) belong to the US fund and LGAM. Under the LR10.1 waiver, LF1 may have more limited redemption rights in the underlying funds than other investors (pdf 113).
- Investor reserve (pdf 67, 69) is a discretionary La Trobe Financial reserve for 12 Month Term Account investors, not an LF1 asset or guarantee.
- The 'never suspended or withheld payments' history claim (pdf 68) is about the Australian Credit Fund, not LF1, which had no history at the PDS date (pdf 73).
- Account name changed. The PDS (May 2025) says '12 Month Term Account'; the FY2026 annual report and later documents say '12 Month Investment Account (12MIA)'. They look like the same account, but confirm (e.g. by ARSN 088 178 321) before matching values across documents.
- The pro forma balance sheet at 26 May 2025 (pdf 95-96) is all cash ($100m/$300m) and shows no underlying investment. Do not use it as LF1's investment in the underlying funds; use the FY2026 annual report note 8 for 30 Jun 2026.
- Source inconsistency in the PDS. EY's letter (pdf 99) says up to 1,500,000 units for $300m; sections 13.2 and 16.2 (pdf 95, 110) say 150,000,000 units at $2.00. Good test case for the consistency checker.
- IPO costs (1.75% cornerstone fee, JLM fees up to 3%, legal etc.) are paid by the Manager, not by LF1 (pdf 89, 90, 107, 112). Do not add them to LF1's ICR.
- Transaction costs of 0.02% include the US fund's FX hedging and LGAM repurchase costs (pdf 81, 86). They are underlying costs shown at LF1 level.
- Dated statements become stale. 'No litigation' (pdf 113, at 26 May 2025) and 'over 550 staff' (pdf 67, 73) are as at the PDS date. The FY2026 annual report (pdf 9) mentions an ASIC interim stop order dated 18 Sep 2025 on 12MIA and US fund Class B target market determinations. That is after the PDS date and at the UNDERLYING level, not LF1.
- Valuation timing. LF1 NAV relies on monthly values supplied by the underlying funds (pdf 66, 74). LF1's NAV at a month-end can therefore lag, and an LF1 month-end NAV is not on the same basis as the ASX price.
- Two underlying funds, not one: LF1 is easy to read as a feeder into the 12MIA only. The TMD p1, FAQs p1/p3, profile p1-p5 and deck p3/p5 all say LF1 invests in the 12MIA AND La Trobe US Private Credit Fund Class A. At 31 Aug 2026 USPC was the LARGER holding (58.79% vs 41.01%). Any 'LF1 = 12MIA' mapping misstates the fund's risk. The LTACF annual report covers only ~41% of LF1's assets.
- 'Underlying Fund' changes meaning by document. In the TMD p1/p4, 'Underlying Funds' means the 12MIA and USPC. In the monthly profile p6 'Important Information', 'Fund' means La Trobe US Private Credit Fund and 'Underlying Fund' means LGAM Private Credit LLC. Elsewhere 'Fund' means LF1. An extractor keyed on 'the Fund' or 'Underlying Fund' will assign values to the wrong layer.
- Account naming: the TMD and FAQs say '12 Month Term Account'; the profile and deck say '12 Month Investment Account' (12MIA). These appear to be the same account, since the ARSN and allocation range match, but confirm against the PDS before treating them as one field.
- The fee label hides the scope: monthly_profile p3 labels a row 'Management Fee' but the value is 'estimated management fees and costs' of 1.93% p.a. of LF1 NTA. That is likely a total-cost figure (possibly including underlying fund costs), not a base management fee. Do not compare it with a PDS base management fee rate. None of the four docs mentions a performance fee, so PDS performance-fee wording probably describes underlying-fund or USPC-level fees; this needs checking against the PDS, not assuming.
- Underlying returns presented next to LF1: deck p5 12MIA 6.75% p.a. (current 1 Jul 2026, after 12MIA fees) and USPC Class A 7.50% (as at 1 Jan 2026, before FX). Deck p6 12MIA 1/3/5-yr returns of 6.54/6.70/6.04% and the outperformance-since-2002 claims are underlying-fund figures, not LF1 performance. LF1 only started on 24 Jun 2025.
- Three different AUMs: the La Trobe group $26bn (deck p4, manager level); LTACF $14.9bn (deck p14, all accounts of the underlying fund); and the 12MIA $11.7bn (deck p14), $11,627m AUM vs $11,533m loan portfolio (profile p4, 31 Aug 2026) and $11,513m loan portfolio (deck p8, 30 Sep 2026).
